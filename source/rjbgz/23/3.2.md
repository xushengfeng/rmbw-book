Two of the most exciting, innovative, and versatile technologies are virtual reality (VR) and its close cousin, augmented reality (AR). Virtual reality is where a person enters into a digital world, usually via a 3-D headset. Augmented reality can take several different forms, but it involves placing virtual overlays over a real scene. To see how these technologies work, let us look at the experiences of Li Dongshan, a 72-year-old widower from rural Shandong.

As a present from his son, Li received high-speed train tickets to tour China. However, Li had to abort the trip after he suffered a heart attack and ended up needing bypass surgery in Beijing. In the old days, bypass surgery required opening up the patient’s chest and ribs. It would leave a huge scar and recovery could take more than two months. Li was lucky, however, as his surgeon used the latest technology. He began by inserting a tiny camera and small surgical instruments through 5-millimetre holes made in Li’s chest. He then put on a special pair of AR glasses and stood over Li. As he looked down at Li’s chest, a realistic 3-D model of Li’s heart and the surgical instruments were projected onto the lenses of the AR glasses, along with all of Li’s vital signs. Using VR gloves, the doctor then manipulated the surgical instruments. He was not really touching the instruments when he did so, but rather, his hand motions were transmitted to a computer, and the computer told the instruments what to do. And even though he was not really touching anything, the gloves gave the surgeon the same feeling as though the surgical tools were in the palms of his hands. The image on his lenses also displayed his hands as though they were inside Li’s chest using the surgical instruments.

Using this technology, the operation was a roaring success. Even though Li was in great pain before the operation, he suffered only very minor pain afterwards and felt better than he had in years. However, as a precaution, Li still needed to stay two weeks in hospital, and so his tour of China had to be cancelled. At the same time, the doctors wanted him to be up and walking about to help him heal faster. As it turned out, the rehabilitation centre at the hospital had facilities for VR world tours. This amounted to a platform that Li stood on and could walk on in any direction. The surface of the platform moved with his feet, so in truth he was not really walking anywhere. However, he wore a VR headset and VR gloves, so he had the illusion that he was walking through a city. When a virtual van drove by, Li was shocked to hear the engine, see the ground shaking, and feel the wind blowing in his face. He was even able to reach out and pick up an apple in a market and smell fragrant flowers.

Using VR, he walked through Rome and enjoyed its ancient ruins. He travelled to Machu Picchu in Peru to experience a terrific sunrise, climbed to the summit of Qomolangma, and then strolled along the rim of the Grand Canyon. He was also able to visit many of the places that he had planned to tour in China, such as the Temple of Heaven, the Great Wall, and the Terracotta Army. But his most interesting trip was to the Louvre Museum in Paris, because with his VR gloves he was able to do something which is usually impossible in real life: He could pick up the various works of art, closely examine them, and touch them with his hands.

Li was also able to have virtual visitors. For example, wearing AR glasses, he played board games with his granddaughter, Bingbing, in Shandong. When he looked through the glasses, an image of Bingbing was projected onto the lenses, showing her sitting in the chair opposite him, with a virtual board game in front of her. Using VR gloves, he could move the game pieces. The gloves made it seem as though he was actually touching them, and even as though he was actually patting Bingbing’s head. Naturally, this is exactly what he saw through the glasses as well, even though Bingbing was many hundred kilometres away.

Near the end of his hospital stay, his friends held a virtual birthday party for him. Using his AR glasses, he could see and talk to them, and enjoy their company. However, as soon as he took the glasses off, the room was empty once again. He realised that while VR and AR technology was not quite the same as real life, it had nonetheless enriched his life in many ways.
